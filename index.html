Source note 01 / Translation integrity

A five-line audit for a translated exam question

A translated exam question asks the reader to trust two things at once. The mathematics must survive transcription, and the English must preserve the original conditions.

  1. Name the paper, year, subject, and question number.
  2. Keep a reference to the Chinese source wording.
  3. Record the English version that the learner actually saw.
  4. State who reviewed the translation and which review state applies.
  5. Give readers a correction route tied to the exact question.

The list does not prove that a translation is perfect. It makes a possible error locatable. A report such as “Question 12 drops the word ‘distinct’” can be checked. A report saying only that the paper feels odd cannot.

GaokaoExam.org shows why the distinction matters. Its featured experience currently presents 21 source-linked questions from 2026 Shanghai Mathematics. The site describes the material as independently reconstructed and translated, and it says clearly that the result is not an official government publication or official English edition.

Keep corrections visible after publication. Record what changed and when. A learner who worked from an earlier version can then understand whether the change affected style, notation, a condition, or the answer itself.
